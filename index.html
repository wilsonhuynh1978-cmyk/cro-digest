The Skill Trap: When Letting AI Cook Means Trusting Strangers

Over 35% of 4,000 public agent skills have security flaws — one in eight is critical. Agent skills are only as good as the expertise and boundaries encoded into them: the real risk isn't the model's hallucination but the unseen dependencies and untrusted third-party code that a skill can execute, making security and vetting as important as the prompt. Synthesized from IBM Technology, Y Combinator.

Science: The Uncontainable: AI, Fiction, and Friction

Containment is not possible, but it has to be possible, because otherwise it contains us. AI's omni-use nature and the paradox of control: the same technology that can find tumors can target battlefield targets, making effective containment both impossible and essential, while competitive pressures drive every organization to race for intelligence regardless of risk. Synthesized from DOAC, JRE, StarTalk.

Vital Signs: Reprogramming, Rapamycin, and the Credibility Crisis

This week, the first human was dosed in a trial that could prove age reversal is real—while the man who told you how to live longer just had his credibility explode. The first FDA-cleared in vivo reprogramming trial in humans (Sinclair/Life Biosciences) signals a shift from animal models to human testing, but the science is far from settled: the Rapamycin + exercise RCT shows that even promising drugs can fail to outperform lifestyle, reinforcing that exercise — not any drug — remains the proven foundation for longevity.

Longview: The Balance Sheet of Empire: Debt, Drones, and the Cost of Stability

The world's wealth just hit $600 trillion—but three-quarters of it is a mirage, and nearly a third is debt that could explode at any moment. The global economy runs on a fragile fiction: net worth has inflated through asset price bubbles and debt rather than productive investment, so the entire system is exposed to a balance-sheet reckoning that would hit households, states, and strategic rivals alike. Synthesized from Economics Explained, Predictive History, Warographics.
